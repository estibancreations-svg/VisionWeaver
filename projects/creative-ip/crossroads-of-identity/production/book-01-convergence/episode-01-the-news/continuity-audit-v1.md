## 3. BIG DECISIONS (these change the story, so they're Sire's call)

### D1. Tampa or Atlanta? The biggest gap.
The script, all the Atlanta sets and every lock sheet put Marcus's firm and condo in **Atlanta**. But the **chapter** and the **Character Histories** still put them in **Tampa**:
- Chapter: "The Westshore Financial District … Tampa's cloudless blue sky," "artists from Tampa's East Side," and "twenty-three floors above the Tampa streets."
- Histories: Marcus "returned to Tampa" in 2016, has a "downtown Tampa condo," and is "known but guarded" in "Tampa's Black gay community."

It's like a map with two different addresses for the same house: readers of the book and viewers of the show would get different stories.

**Options:**
- **A. Atlanta everywhere (recommended).** Change about five lines in the chapter and the 2016–2023 part of Marcus's history. After Howard Law in D.C., he took a job with an Atlanta firm. The distance also explains why his visits to Elijah thinned out. One story, one truth.
- **B. Atlanta for the show only.** Keep the book in Tampa and log it as an "adaptation difference." It's less work, but two versions of Marcus's life have to be tracked forever.
- **C. Tampa everywhere.** Every Atlanta set (conference room, office, bathroom, condo) would have to be rebuilt. Not recommended.

If A, the lines that need new wording are listed in Appendix A.

### D2. The house's floor plan: the Histories and our built sets disagree
| Room | Character Histories say | Built sets + Location Bible say |
|---|---|---|
| Bookstore "Heritage Pages" | Ground floor, since 1972 | Not placed. The parlor record even calls it "later" a bookstore. |
| Community meeting room | Ground floor, behind the bookstore | The south front parlor |
| Kitchen | **2nd floor** (communal) | **Ground floor, west side** (golden evening light, back porch) |
| Living room | 2nd floor | Not built |
| Garden | "Small courtyard garden hidden from street view" | The enclosed, private back yard (same idea, different name) |
| Basement | Converted by Elijah into living space | A dim storage cellar with small high windows |

**Recommended fix: make the Histories match what's built.** The south parlor **is Heritage Pages** and has been since 1972. Its tall bookshelves are already in the plate. The LGBTQ+ section sits behind a beaded curtain, and the shop doubles as the meeting room after hours. **Customers come in through the small side portico on the south wall**, which is already in Sire's set house image, so the front door and hall stay the family's private way in. Elijah's office sits behind the shop. The kitchen stays on the ground floor. The 2nd floor has bedrooms and the living room. The 3rd floor has bedrooms, the small library and, later, Desiree's studio. The back yard is the "courtyard garden."

**About the basement, a real-world note:** houses in Florida rarely have basements, because the ground water sits close to the surface and a basement floods easily (Maronda Homes, n.d.). A lived-in basement bedroom is hard to believe. **Recommended:** keep it a small, damp, half-sunk storage cellar (the plate we have) and remove "converted into living space" from the Histories.

### D3. When and by whom the house was built
- The Histories say it was **built in 1908 for a wealthy cigar factory owner**.
- The Bible and our records say "late 1890s to early 1900s" (1908 fits inside that).
- The chapter says **"the family who'd built it"** carried a memory from the Black Seminole towns.

The catch: a 1908 cigar factory owner in Ybor City would most likely have been Spanish or Cuban, not a family carrying Black Seminole memory, so the two lines can read as a contradiction.

**Options:**
- **A. (My suggestion, a new idea for Sire to accept or reject.)** Lock **1908**. The house was built *for* a cigar factory owner *by* a Black family of carpenters and masons, and they are "the family who'd built it." This matches the chapter's exact words, keeps the Histories line, and gives the hidden room a quiet possible explanation (the builders put it in). It stays unproven, as the Bible requires.
- **B.** Lock 1908 and drop "for a cigar factory owner" from the Histories.
- **C.** Leave both as they are.

If 1908 is locked, one small chapter line would change: "rising out of what had been swampland **a few years** before" becomes "**not so many years** before." Ybor City was founded in 1885, 23 years earlier.

## 4. SMALL FIXES (clear mistakes with an obvious answer; applied as one batch if Sire approves)

**Chapter (v5)**
1. **Typo:** "the way he'd started to notice it noticing it" → "the way he'd started noticing it."
2. **Grammar:** "his backpack clutching a hastily packed collection" (this says the backpack is doing the clutching) → "his backpack clutched to his chest, stuffed with a hastily packed collection." This also matches the FL-MR15 lock (the backpack is clutched at his chest).

**Character Histories (becomes v2)**
3. **Arrival season:** "summer of 2006" → "fall of 2006." *(Already approved by Sire.)*
4. **Pride history** is wrong in five places. What really happened: Tampa Pride **stopped after 2002**, when a failed event at Raymond James Stadium left organizers in debt. In **June 2005**, Hillsborough County banned its **own government** from recognizing or promoting Pride. The commission **repealed that ban unanimously on June 5, 2013**, and **Tampa Pride came back in 2015** (DeBruhl, 2022; WUSF, 2013). The Histories' lines "Tampa Pride was banned (2005–2015)," "banned by homophobic county commissioners," "the county had banned Pride events" and "revival … after a ten-year hiatus" get rewritten to match. The chapter already has it right.
5. **Marcus and the 2008 vote:** the Histories say "as a young voter" he saw Amendment 2 pass. He turned 18 in January 2009, so in November 2008 he was **17 and couldn't vote**. The fix: "as a 17-year-old high school senior, too young to vote." (It passed with 61.92%; Ballotpedia, n.d.)
6. **Marcus and Windsor (June 26, 2013):** it says he "was in law school." He had just finished Florida State and hadn't started Howard yet. The fix: "was about to start law school."
7. **Marcus and Pulse (June 12, 2016):** "returned to Florida just months before" → weeks (he'd have finished Howard in May 2016). *This line gets rewritten anyway if D1 = Atlanta.*
8. **Marcus "as a teenager in the early 2000s"** → "in 2006."
9. **Jordan contradicts itself.** One line says Jordan met Elijah "through labor organizing in **2015**," but Jordan's own story says Jordan **arrived in Tampa in 2019** (at 18 in 2015, Jordan was still in Oakland). The fix: **2019**.
10. **Desiree contradicts herself.** One line says she "first encountered Elijah's bookstore as a college student in the late 2000s," but her own story says she **discovered it in 2020**, after New York (she was at RISD in Rhode Island in the late 2000s). The fix: **2020**.
11. **Amara and Pulse** is called a "local tragedy." Pulse was in Orlando, about 85 miles from Tampa. The fix: "an in-state tragedy."
12. **Amara and Elijah:** the summary line says Elijah helped him "navigate his emerging bisexuality," but his own story says Amara "remained guarded about his sexuality" with Elijah. The fix: "gently introduced him to Black LGBTQ+ writers, long before Amara was ready to say anything out loud."
13. **Elijah's GI Bill:** "using money from his GI Bill" → "a GI Bill home loan." The GI Bill backed home loans (the government guaranteed the loan); it didn't hand out cash for houses. *(Low priority, just for accuracy.)*

**Math that follows from ages already locked (nothing new invented)**
14. For Elijah to be **61 in fall 2006** *and* **77 in late March 2022**, his birthday has to fall between **January 1 and about March 20, 1945**. The script's "(77)" at PART 5 S01 then works. Sire can pick an exact date later if it's ever needed on screen.

**Noted but recommended to keep as is**
- "The past he'd spent **seventeen years** trying to outrun." Fall 2006 to January 2023 is about 16 years and 4 months. Most readers count 2023 − 2006 = 17, and it's Sire's original line. **Keep.**
- "The journal … that his father hadn't found and burned along with the texts." It works if the father printed the texts out. **Keep** (Sire's original).
