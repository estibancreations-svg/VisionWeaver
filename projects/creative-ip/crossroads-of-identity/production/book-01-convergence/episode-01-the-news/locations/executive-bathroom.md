# Location — Executive Bathroom (Marcus's Atlanta Law Firm)

Same building/firm as the glass conference room backdrop already approved. Appears in Chapter 1 as the private restroom Marcus retreats to after learning of Elijah's death.

## Research process
Bathroom-specific photography of real executive washrooms at top-tier Atlanta law firms essentially doesn't exist publicly — these are the least-photographed rooms in any office. Instead, the design was built from the documented design language of the surrounding executive tier at real Atlanta AmLaw-caliber firms and comparable Class-A towers, extended reasonably into a private restroom:

- Nelson Mullins Riley & Scarborough's Atlanta office (designed by Smallwood): soft neutral high-contrast palettes, rustic wood flooring, large-scale painted millwork, hospitality-influenced monumental aesthetic.
- Alston & Bird's Atlanta HQ, designed by Carson Guest Inc. — confirms top-tier Atlanta firms hire high-end commercial A&D firms rather than generic build-outs.
- A confidential international law firm project (IA Interior Architects): floor-to-ceiling glass offices, deliberate design response to privacy/confidentiality, private support areas sited away from glass sightlines — directly relevant to how a private executive restroom would be positioned off a partner corridor.
- General executive-washroom design-trade guidance: natural stone (marble, quartzite, granite) as the anchoring material, large-format slab or mosaic stone, hospitality-grade fixtures, chosen to create a "serene environment."
- Class-A Atlanta towers (3399 Peachtree Buckhead; Ten Twenty Spring/Portman Midtown, explicitly described as having "the design, quality and feel of top-flight hotels") confirm the material tier: stone slab, glass, hospitality lighting, skyline glazing.

**No real firm name, building, or specific interior is depicted or copied — this is original art inspired by the documented material/design language above.**

## Design synthesis used for the art
Book-matched marble slab walls and floor (warm grey/white veining, honed finish), dark walnut vanity with a vessel sink, frameless brushed-brass mirror, recessed hospitality-style lighting, floor-to-ceiling glazing with an Atlanta skyline view, frameless glass shower partition with brass hardware. Quiet, monumental, hotel-adjacent palette rather than generic corporate beige.
