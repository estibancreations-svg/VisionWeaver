## 0. How to read this

Think of this sheet like the tape marks on a theater stage. Every actor and every camera has a numbered spot, and they go back to that same spot every time. That keeps faces, rooms and light the same from shot to shot, and from episode to episode.

There are four kinds of locks:

- **Map pins** (letters like `A`, `E`, `B3`) mark where the camera stands. They're drawn in the maps file.
- **Face locks** (codes like `FL-MR15`) say what must never change about a face, and what changes with age.
- **Light locks** (codes like `LK-2006-PM`) say where the light comes from, what color it is, and how the footage is graded.
- **Plates** are the backdrop pictures already built in Runway. Each one has a task ID.

**One honest limit:** I can't open the saved pictures from here. The maps below are built from the written prompts and the Location Bible. If a picture shows a wall or window on the other side from what the map says, flip the map to match the picture. Never regenerate a locked picture to match the map.

## 1. VisionWeaver Shot Standard v1 (LOCKED; full copy at `standards/shot-standard-v1.md`)

This is the house format for every shot in every episode.

| Field | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Shot ID** | `E01-P3-S07` = Episode 1, PART 3, Shot 7 |
| **Plate** | The Runway backdrop task ID, or `NEEDED` if it hasn't been built yet |
| **Pin** | Where the camera stands on the map |
| **Lens** | Full-frame equivalent in mm. 24 = wide room, 35 = walking, 50 = natural eye, 85 = face close-up, 100 macro = small object inserts |
| **Height and angle** | Camera height in feet. Angle is eye-level, low (looking up), or high (looking down) |
| **Move** | Locked (no movement), push-in, pull-out, track (follow), tilt, or handheld |
| **Framing** | WS wide · MS medium (waist up) · MCU chest up · CU face · ECU eyes or detail · INSERT object only · OTS over-the-shoulder |
| **Face lock** | Which face code applies, and the expression for this shot |
| **Light lock** | Which light code applies, plus notes for this shot |
| **Sound** | Key sounds or dialogue |
| **Dual-format safe** | Compose for 16:9 YouTube, but keep the subject inside the **center 9:16 strip** so the same shot can be cropped for TikTok and Instagram without re-shooting. Every shot below follows this rule. |

## 4. Light locks

### Why the season matters: sun direction check
The house's front faces **due east**, and the staircase landing window faces **south** (Location Bible lock). I calculated where the sun sits over Ybor City in the afternoon using NOAA's standard solar-position equations (NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, n.d.):

| Date (2006) | 4:00 PM | 5:00 PM |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 15 | sun in the **southwest** (230°), 21° high | 239°, 10° high |
| Apr 15 | west-southwest (250°), 50° high | 260°, 37° high |
| Jun 15 | **due west** (271°), 56° high | 277°, 43° high |
| Oct 15 | **southwest** (234°), 36° high | 245°, 25° high |

**What this means:** In fall and winter, the late-afternoon sun is low in the southwest. It shines straight through the south landing window and rakes down the stairs, which gives the "green and amber across the stairs" moment from the chapter. In summer, the sun is high and in the west, so the south window would barely light up. **That's why the arrival is locked to fall or winter.**

### LK-ATL-DAY: Atlanta present day (office and executive bathroom)
- **Time:** midday, January 2023.
- **Key light:** soft, cool daylight (5600K) through the floor-to-ceiling glass on the bathroom's west wall. With the sun in the south at midday, the glass gives even, shadowless side light, with no hard sunbeams.
- **Top light:** warm 3000K recessed hotel-style downlights. They create slight shadows under the brow, which help the moment when his "eyes belong to fifteen-year-old Marcus."
- **Fill:** bounce off the white and grey marble. Keep it low as he breaks down so the contrast rises. Ease it back up when his composure returns (S27).
- **Grade:** neutral-cool, crisp, clean blacks, no grain. This is Attorney Reynolds's world: controlled.

### LK-2006-PM: flashback arrival, late afternoon, fall or winter 2006
- **Time:** 4:00–4:45 PM. The sun is southwest and low, and the sky is "just beginning to go gold at the edges."
- **Exterior (locked plate `2aa055ef…`):** the east front is in **open shade**, lit by the sky with a warm bounce off the ground. Direct gold sun hits only the **south-side wraparound porch** and the top of the turret spire. Camera pin X1 (the southeast corner) shows both sides. **Check this on your saved picture:** the side that looks sunlit should read as the south side.
- **Entrance hall:** the sidelights and transom on the east door **glow** soft and cool with colored glass, but no sunbeams come through them. The warm light comes from the **south**: a warm pool spilling through the parlor archway, and green-and-amber beams from the landing window raking down flight 1. The brass pendant light is off. The interior should look **warmer than the outside**, as if the house itself is glowing.
- **Staircase (locked plate `65584fba…`):** the landing window is the brightest thing in the frame. Colored light patterns fall across the stair treads.
- **Third-floor room (plate `66cbab09…`):** the dormer faces **west** (LOCKED). Low gold sun comes straight in and lays a warm beam across the floor and quilt. When Elijah says "safe house," both faces are in warm light.
- **Grade:** the "memory" look. Warmer, slightly lifted blacks, soft glow around the highlights, fine film-style grain, and greens slightly muted everywhere except the stained glass.

### LK-2022-AM: PART 5 porch (reference only)
- **Time:** late morning, spring 2022. Soft east light, low across the porch, as the chapter describes.
- **Grade:** a memory look, but less warm than 2006. It's a "recent" memory, not a childhood one.
